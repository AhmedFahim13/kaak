Kaak for the browser: privacy policy

Version 0.1.0 · Last updated 27 October 2026 · Publisher: Fahim Ahmed · Contact: theoslab.dev@gmail.com

In one sentence: Kaak doesn't collect, send or sell anything. Everything he keeps stays in your browser on your computer, and the extension makes no internet connections at all.

What Kaak is

Kaak is a browser extension with a cartoon crow who interrupts mindless scrolling on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube (and X or Reddit, only if you switch them on). It works in Chromium browsers such as Microsoft Edge, Opera, Chrome, Brave and Vivaldi.

What Kaak looks at, and what he never reads

Your browser says Kaak can "read and change your data" on the feed sites. That's the only way an extension can draw a crow on a page. What Kaak actually uses on those sites:

  • That you scrolled, and when: mouse wheel, trackpad, touch swipes and scrolling keys, as timestamps and counts.
  • Whether a key was a scrolling key or a typing key. The key is looked at only to sort it into one of those two; which letter it was is never kept. Typing in a comment box only tells Kaak "typing happened".
  • Which kind of page you're on (a Short, a Reel, a feed, a chat), read from the page address while the page is open, so Kaak knows whether it's a feed and when a new video starts. The address is not stored or sent anywhere.
  • Whether the tab is in front and whether your screen is locked, so only real feed time counts.

Kaak never reads posts, captions, comments, names, profiles, messages, images or anything you type, and never takes screenshots. He never runs on chat pages (Messenger, Instagram and TikTok messages), YouTube Studio, Business Suite or YouTube Music, and never on any site that isn't one of the feeds above.

If you pick a learning site for a "bribe" (for example Duolingo or Khan Academy), your browser first asks you to allow that one site. Kaak then learns only whether that site's tab is in front, every few seconds, until the bribe is done.

Sharing

Nothing leaves your computer unless you do it yourself. "Share my stats" downloads a file to your computer, and "Report a problem" copies text for you to paste into an email. You decide whether to send either. Kaak doesn't use the browser's sync storage, analytics, ads, trackers, cookies or any server.

Redirects during a nap or a theft

When Kaak "eats" a feed after your daily limit (a 20-minute nap), or holds a feed he stole, your browser sends the feed sites to Kaak's own page until the timer ends or you win the chase. These rules apply only to the feed sites, never to chat or work pages, and are removed as soon as the timer ends or you remove the extension.

One thing a site could learn

For those redirects to work, the browser requires Kaak's nap page to be reachable by the feed sites. That means a feed site could check whether that page exists and learn that Kaak is installed. It can't learn anything else from it.

Deleting everything

Remove Kaak from your browser's extensions page. That deletes everything he stored and every redirect rule.

Children

Kaak doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes (for example if optional sync with Kaak's phone app is ever added), the new version will be published here before the extension changes, with a new date at the top. Sync, if it comes, will be off by default.
